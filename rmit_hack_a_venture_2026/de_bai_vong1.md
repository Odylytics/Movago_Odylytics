## Background & Problem Statement

Vietnam's financial system is undergoing one of its most significant periods of digital transformation. Rapid advances in digital banking, cashless payments, artificial intelligence (AI), and financial technologies are reshaping how individuals, businesses, and institutions access and deliver financial services. According to the State Bank of Vietnam (SBV), by the end of 2025, nearly 89% of Vietnamese citizens aged 15 and above owned a payment account, while many financial institutions processed more than 95% of their transactions through digital channels. In the first six months of 2026, Vietnam recorded over 15 billion cashless payment transactions, a 34.28% increase compared with the same period in 2025. These achievements demonstrate Vietnam's strong momentum toward becoming a modern digital financial economy.

As the financial ecosystem becomes increasingly interconnected, the challenges facing the sector have also become more complex. Expanding financial access must be accompanied by modern and interoperable financial infrastructure, stronger institutional trust, responsible governance, and greater resilience against emerging risks. The rapid growth of digital financial services has also increased exposure to online fraud and cyber threats. To strengthen payment security, the State Bank of Vietnam introduced the SIMO (Information System for Supporting Management, Monitoring and Prevention of Payment Fraud Risks) fraud prevention system. By the end of July 2026, SIMO had issued about 4.9 million alerts to customers, prompting around 1.6 million customers to suspend or cancel suspicious transactions and helping prevent approximately VND 5.5 trillion from being transferred to fraudulent accounts. These developments highlight that technological advancement alone is insufficient without ensuring security, transparency, and public confidence.

Beyond digital transformation, sustainability has become an increasingly important priority within Vietnam's financial sector. According to the State Bank of Vietnam, outstanding green credit reached more than VND 780 trillion by the end of 2025, supporting projects related to renewable energy, clean agriculture, environmental protection, and sustainable infrastructure. Nevertheless, green credit still accounted for only about 4.2% of the country's total outstanding loans, highlighting significant opportunities to strengthen sustainable finance, improve ESG transparency, and encourage greater investment in environmentally and socially responsible initiatives.

At the same time, Vietnam remains one of the countries most vulnerable to climate change, exposing financial institutions and businesses to increasing physical and transition risks. The World Bank's Vietnam Country Climate and Development Report (CCDR) estimates that without adequate adaptation and resilience measures, climate change could cost Vietnam about 12-14.5% of GDP per year by 2050, while climate-related shocks will continue to affect infrastructure, agriculture, supply chains, insurance, investment, and financial stability. These challenges reinforce the growing importance of integrating climate resilience into financial decision-making and long-term economic planning.

Recognizing these opportunities and challenges, RMIT Hack-A-Venture 2026 adopts the theme "Open Innovation for a Resilient and Sustainable Financial System in Vietnam" This theme reflects the belief that many of today's financial challenges cannot be solved by a single organization, discipline, or technology alone. Instead, meaningful innovation requires collaboration across academia, industry, government, startups, and communities while leveraging emerging digital technologies such as Artificial Intelligence (AI), Data Analytics, Distributed Systems, Blockchain, the Internet of Things (IoT), and other innovative technologies. Through the principles of Open Innovation, participants are encouraged to combine diverse expertise, publicly available resources, and interdisciplinary perspectives to develop practical solutions that address real-world financial challenges.

Participants are challenged to develop innovative solutions that address meaningful challenges within Vietnam's financial system by aligning their ideas with one of the competition's five pillars:

1. Financial Inclusion & Economic Enablement
2. Financial Infrastructure & Ecosystem Interoperability
3. Sustainable Finance & ESG Integrity
4. Climate Risk Management & Financial Resilience
5. Trust Architecture & Financial Integrity

Each team is expected to select one primary pillar as the focus of its solution. However, interdisciplinary proposals that meaningfully address the intersection of two or more pillars are highly encouraged, provided that a clear primary pillar remains the central focus of the proposed solution.

RMIT Hack-A-Venture 2026 also encourages participants to develop innovations that contribute to one or more of the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs), demonstrating how technological innovation can create meaningful economic, social, and environmental impact while strengthening Vietnam's financial ecosystem.

## Detail Five Pillars with Examples

*Note: Those 10 examples below are intended as illustrative case studies to help participants understand the scope of each pillar. They are not prescribed solution directions, and teams are encouraged to develop original ideas that address Vietnam's financial challenges within the chosen pillar.*

### Pillar 1: Financial Inclusion & Economic Enablement

**Pillar Overview:**

A resilient financial system should enable individuals, businesses, and communities to participate meaningfully in economic activities, regardless of their socioeconomic background (their income level, education, or social circumstances) or geographic location. However, unequal access to financial services, limited economic opportunities, and disparities in financial knowledge continue to prevent many people from fully benefiting from the formal financial ecosystem (regulated financial services such as banks, licensed lenders, insurers, and investment providers).

This pillar focuses on addressing barriers that restrict financial participation and economic empowerment. Participants are encouraged to explore challenges related to financial accessibility, equitable resource allocation (ensuring financial resources and opportunities are distributed fairly based on needs and circumstances), and informed financial decision-making. Solutions may seek to improve how financial services are accessed, how capital and opportunities are distributed, or how individuals and organizations make financial decisions based on reliable information.

Rather than concentrating on specific financial products, this pillar emphasizes creating a more inclusive financial ecosystem where individuals and businesses can participate with greater confidence, accessibility, and fairness. By enabling broader participation and reducing systemic inequalities (long-standing disadvantages caused by structural or institutional factors rather than individual choices), innovations under this pillar can contribute to sustainable economic growth and strengthen the resilience of Vietnam's financial system.

### Pillar 2: Financial Infrastructure & Ecosystem Interoperability

**Pillar Overview:**

Modern financial systems rely on interconnected infrastructures (digital systems and technologies that enable financial organizations to exchange information and deliver services) that enable institutions, businesses, and consumers to exchange information, process transactions, and deliver financial services efficiently. As financial ecosystems continue to evolve, challenges such as fragmented systems, limited interoperability (the ability of different systems or organizations to communicate and work together seamlessly), operational inefficiencies, and legacy technologies can hinder collaboration, increase costs, and reduce overall system performance.

This pillar focuses on strengthening the underlying infrastructure that supports financial activities across the ecosystem. Participants are encouraged to explore ways to improve connectivity between stakeholders, streamline financial operations, enhance system scalability, and facilitate secure and efficient information exchange. The pillar also considers how emerging technologies can modernize financial processes and enable greater coordination among banks, fintech companies, enterprises, regulators, and other ecosystem participants (organizations that contribute to financial services).

By improving interoperability and operational efficiency, innovations developed under this pillar can contribute to a more connected, adaptive, and resilient financial ecosystem capable of supporting future technological advancements and increasing demands within Vietnam's digital economy.

### Pillar 3: Sustainable Finance & ESG Integrity

**Pillar Overview:**

As sustainability becomes an increasingly important consideration in financial decision-making, financial institutions, businesses, and investors face growing expectations to demonstrate responsible practices, transparent reporting, and measurable long-term impact. At the same time, inconsistent disclosures, limited accountability, and concerns surrounding greenwashing (making misleading or exaggerated claims about environmental sustainability) continue to challenge the credibility of sustainability initiatives.

This pillar focuses on promoting responsible finance by improving transparency, accountability, and integrity in environmental, social, and governance (ESG) practices. Participants are encouraged to explore challenges related to sustainability reporting, responsible investment, impact measurement, and the verification of ESG claims. Solutions may seek to strengthen trust in sustainable finance by improving the quality, reliability, and accessibility of sustainability-related information throughout the financial ecosystem.

Rather than viewing sustainability as solely an environmental objective, this pillar recognizes that long-term financial resilience depends on balancing economic performance with responsible governance and broader societal outcomes. Innovations in this area can help strengthen market confidence while supporting Vietnam's transition toward a more sustainable and transparent financial future.

### Pillar 4: Climate Risk Management & Financial Resilience

**Pillar Overview:**

Climate change and environmental uncertainty are increasingly influencing financial systems by affecting investment decisions, business continuity, infrastructure resilience, and long-term economic stability. Financial institutions and organizations must therefore strengthen their ability to anticipate, assess, and respond to climate-related risks while supporting sustainable economic development.

This pillar focuses on improving the financial sector's capacity to manage environmental risks and enhance long-term resilience. Participants are encouraged to explore challenges involving climate risk assessment, sustainable investment, green finance, and preparedness for future environmental or economic disruptions. Solutions may aim to improve forecasting capabilities, support informed investment decisions, or strengthen resilience planning for financial institutions, businesses, and communities.

By enabling more effective management of climate-related risks and encouraging sustainable financial practices, innovations developed under this pillar can contribute to a financial system that is better prepared for future uncertainties while supporting long-term economic and environmental sustainability.

### Pillar 5: Trust Architecture & Financial Integrity

**Pillar Overview:**

Trust forms the foundation of every financial system. Individuals, businesses, and institutions depend on secure, transparent, and accountable financial processes to confidently exchange value, make investments, and participate in economic activities. As financial systems become increasingly digital and interconnected, maintaining trust requires continuous efforts to address fraud, cybersecurity threats, information asymmetry, and governance challenges.

This pillar focuses on strengthening confidence in financial ecosystems by improving transparency, security, accountability, and institutional integrity. Participants are encouraged to explore challenges related to fraud prevention, financial security, market transparency, governance, and the reliability of financial information. Solutions may seek to enhance the ability of financial systems to detect risks, protect stakeholders, improve transparency, and reinforce public confidence in financial institutions and markets.

By fostering trustworthy financial environments, innovations under this pillar can help create a more resilient financial ecosystem where transactions, institutions, and markets operate with greater integrity, transparency, and public confidence, ultimately supporting sustainable economic development in Vietnam.
