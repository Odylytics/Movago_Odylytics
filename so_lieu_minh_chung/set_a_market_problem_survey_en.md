# Set A · Problem and Market Survey (English)

English version of [Bộ A](bo_a_khao_sat_van_de_thi_truong.md). Administered **before** the respondent sees the demo. Its aim is to show that the problem is real, painful enough to matter, already costs people money or time, and to identify who decides to buy. [Set B](set_b_web_app_evaluation_en.md) measures the rest after hands-on use.

Estimated completion time: **12–15 minutes** (Google Forms). State this in the invitation.

## Study design

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Population | People who process, verify or approve inbound ocean freight invoices or debit notes before payment, at freight forwarders, shipping-line agents or importer/exporter shippers in Vietnam. |
| Sampling | Convenience sample via VLA, HLA, Zalo/Facebook groups, LinkedIn and personal contacts. Record the source channel (S0). Do not describe it as a random sample. |
| Sample size | Target n ≥ 30 valid responses to report percentages; below 30, label results "initial signal". To compare users with buyers, each group needs at least 10. |
| Fieldwork | Record open and close dates. Do not edit questions mid-fieldwork; if unavoidable, report wave 1 and wave 2 separately. |
| Order | Keep the section order. Randomise option order in multiple-choice and ranking questions (use "shuffle" in Google Forms), except "Other", which stays last. |
| Anonymity | Collect no name, email or company name with the responses. Contact details in A2 go to a separate form so they cannot be linked to answers. |
| Currency | Vietnamese dong. For reporting, 1 USD = 25,500 VND (09/11/2026), as in the [project brief](../colio_ai_project_brief.md). |

### Invitation and consent

> Team Odylytics (university students) is studying how accountants check freight invoices before paying them, to enter the KTC Ideathon and to assess whether a support tool is feasible. The survey takes about 12–15 minutes and collects no names or company names. Answers are used only in aggregate in our report and slides. Data is kept for at most 12 months, and you may ask us to delete your response. Participation is voluntary and you may stop at any time.

**C0.** Do you agree to take part under these conditions? · Single choice: Yes · No (end of form).

## Section 2 · Current practice, measured on specific events

Form instruction: "For the next questions, think of the **most recent freight invoice** you checked, or **last month**. Please do not give a general estimate."

| # | Question | Type | Options / scale | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Last month, roughly how many freight invoices or debit notes did your team receive? | Single choice | Under 100 · 100–500 · 500–1,000 · 1,000–3,000 · Over 3,000 · Not sure | Scale; the 1,000 invoices/month scenario. |
| H2 | For the most recent freight invoice: how many minutes of **hands-on work** (opening documents, comparing, recording), excluding waiting for others? | Single choice | Under 5 · 5–10 · 10–20 · 20–30 · Over 30 | Replace the assumed 15-minute figure with the survey median. |

## Section 3 · Pain level and priority

Scale 1–7: 1 = Strongly disagree · 7 = Strongly agree.

| # | Question | Type |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | "Reconciling freight invoices against shipment records and approved rates takes a lot of effort in my team." | Scale 1–7 |
| P2 | "The risk of overpaying or paying twice for freight is a real concern for my company." | Scale 1–7 |
| P3 | "Our current reconciliation process is good enough and does not need improvement." | Scale 1–7 (reverse-keyed) |
| P4 | Rank these accounts-payable difficulties from hardest (1) to least hard (5). | Ranking, 5 items shuffled: Reconciling freight invoices to shipments and rates · Collecting documents from several channels · Tracking due dates and cash flow · Reconciling supplier statements · Keying data into accounting software |
| P5 | Has the company ever tried to improve how freight invoices are checked (software, outsourcing, process change)? | Single choice: Implemented · Tried and abandoned · Considering · No |
| P6 | If "Tried and abandoned" or "Implemented": what was missing, or why was it dropped? | Short answer. Optional. Shown for the first two P5 options. |

P1 and P3 are a consistency pair. A respondent giving 7 to both is flagged during data cleaning.

## Section 4 · Pricing and the buying decision

Before this section, show a neutral three-line description of the tool. Do **not** state quantified benefits ("saves X hours") and do **not** state Movago's price.

Form instruction: "Assume a tool that checks freight invoices by matching documents to the shipment and rate card, showing discrepancies with evidence, and exporting a report, for about 500 invoices per month." Answers are numeric, in **VND per month**.

| # | Question | Type |
|---|---|---|
| V1 | At what monthly price would the tool be **so cheap** that you would doubt its quality? | Number, VND/month |
| V2 | At what monthly price would it be **a bargain**: cheap but you still trust it? | Number |
| V3 | At what monthly price would it start to feel **expensive**, but you would still consider it? | Number |
| V4 | At what monthly price would it be **too expensive** to buy at all? | Number |

Logic check: V1 ≤ V2 ≤ V3 ≤ V4. Responses that break the order are excluded from the price analysis only.

## Data cleaning

1. Remove "Outside the industry" (S1), "No" (S2) and non-consent (C0).
2. Remove responses completed in under 3 minutes (or under 40% of the median time).
3. Flag straight-lining (same answer across 15 consecutive scale items) and P1/P3 inconsistency.
4. Exclude from price analysis responses that break V1 ≤ V2 ≤ V3 ≤ V4.
5. Report: responses received, removed per reason, valid n, response rate if the number of invitations is known.

## Analysis and reporting

| Metric | Method | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Sample profile | Frequency tables S0–S8 | State that it is a convenience sample. |
| Scale of the problem | Median and interquartile range of H1, H2, H3, H5 | Medians, because data are skewed. Use group midpoints. |
| Share who had errors | "Yes" in H7 / answered H7, with 95% Wilson interval | Report numerator and denominator. |
| Pain level | Mean and SD of P1, P2; P3 reverse-scored when combined; share choosing 5–7 | Report the distribution, not only the mean. |
| Priority ranking | Mean rank of P4 and share ranking it first | Where reconciliation sits relative to other tasks. |
| Acceptable price range | Van Westendorp intersections: point of marginal cheapness, optimal price point, point of marginal expensiveness | Valid responses only; report n. Use only respondents with purchasing influence (S4 ≠ "Not involved") for price conclusions. |
| Buyers | Frequencies of B1, B2, B3 | For the business-model slide. |
| Barriers | Frequencies of R5, means of R1, R3, R4 | For the Q&A appendix. |
| Commitment | Share choosing "Show masked invoices" or higher in A1 | The strongest demand indicator. |
| Group comparison | Users vs. budget approvers (S4): compare P1, A1 | Below 10 per group, describe only; do not infer differences. |

## Reporting rules

- Attach n to every percentage: "62% (18/29)".
- Keep three kinds of number apart: **survey figures**, **simulated figures** (case `INV-095`, the 1,000 invoices scenario) and **pilot figures** (none yet).
- Replace the assumed 15 minutes in the ROI scenario with the H2 median when real data exist, labelled "per survey, n = …".
- If n < 30, write "initial signal", not "results show".
- Limitations section: convenience sample; recall error from self-report; the price range reflects stated intent, not purchase behaviour.
